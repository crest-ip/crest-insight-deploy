What are we actually getting for our money?

Part 5 of 5

We no longer produce models

The heading is not literally true.

The team still produces models. We describe capabilities, processes, information and technology. We need these perspectives to understand consequences and create direction.

But we no longer describe model production as our mission.

Our mission is to help the organisation make better decisions and carry out change with a greater understanding of the consequences.

Models are among our most important means.

They are not the goal.

The same work, a different starting point

The difference is visible in how we prioritise.

A request does not automatically receive high priority because a senior stakeholder wants a map. We ask which decision it will support.

A technically interesting analysis has to wait if another question carries greater business risk.

And we stop when the evidence is sufficient for the decision, even if the model could be refined much further.

That is not always easy.

I lead a team of people who enjoy understanding complex relationships. They see opportunities to improve, extend and deepen almost every model.

That is a strength.

But it is also my responsibility to help them decide when further work no longer creates enough value.

The difference is also visible in how we follow up our work.

We do not merely count deliverables.

We try to capture where architecture knowledge made a risk visible, changed a direction, was reused in the next initiative or shortened the time required to produce reliable decision support.

That is not always easy to measure.

And we cannot automatically attribute the entire value of a successful decision to the architecture work.

But we can show how our knowledge contributed.

That is still more relevant than the number of diagrams.

AI changes capacity, not responsibility

Agents have changed how the team works.

They can help us read more material, identify possible relationships, compare different descriptions and prepare analyses considerably faster than before.

But greater capacity makes prioritisation more important, not less.

If we can produce ten times as many models without knowing which questions they answer, we have not become ten times more valuable.

We have merely become faster at creating a new quality-assurance backlog.

That is why we need a shared foundation.

Prime Arch can provide our agents with an ontology containing defined types of business objects and relationships. It helps them distinguish between processes, capabilities, information and applications, for example, and work with consistent modelling rules.

But we still have to build, review and manage our own organisational knowledge.

That knowledge contains our actual business objects, relationships, sources and judgements.

Only when agents have access to both the shared ontology and relevant organisational knowledge can they help us work in a more connected way.

This does not mean that everyone has to use the same agent or the same tool.

It means that we need to be able to reuse the same knowledge and test new conclusions against the same conceptual foundation.

It does not replace my responsibility to set direction, prioritise, say no and decide what quality the situation requires.

If anything, it makes that responsibility clearer.

A different answer to management

If I am asked today what the organisation has gained from its architecture work, I do not begin with our models.

I begin with the decisions.

I explain which risks became visible before they became expensive. Which initiatives could be coordinated because their dependencies were known. Which choices changed, and which changes could proceed with clearer conditions.

Then I can show the knowledge behind those answers.

I can show which business objects and relationships the analysis was based on, which sources supported the conclusions and where uncertainty still remained.

That does not mean that we are always right.

But it does mean that our work can be understood, reviewed and used again.

And that is an important part of building an architecture function that creates value over time.

I do not need management to understand all of our models.

I need them to understand which decisions we help them make.
