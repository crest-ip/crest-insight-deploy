Where ARC complements ServiceNow

Part 2 of 5

A shared model becomes more valuable when its boundary is explicit

Shared models solve a real enterprise problem. They give teams common objects, relationships and controls with which to coordinate work. ServiceNow’s Common Service Data Model provides a standardized framework for relating business and technical service information, while Enterprise Architecture extends the platform’s ability to connect applications, data, processes and business capabilities.

The value is clear. So is the architectural question that follows: where does the shared model end?

An extensible platform has no natural stopping point. More entities can be represented. More sources can be connected. More workflows can depend on the resulting information. Without an explicit boundary, technical possibility can gradually become assumed authority.

Own, represent or reference

Every important information object used in a shared model should have one of three relationships to the platform.

Owned. The platform governs the record’s lifecycle, accountability and operational use. It is the authoritative source for the relevant purpose or decision.

Represented. The platform holds or exposes a useful view of knowledge that remains authoritative elsewhere. The representation needs identifiable provenance, sufficient freshness and a route back to its authority.

Referenced. The platform knows that relevant knowledge exists elsewhere and can connect users or workflows to it without reproducing its full meaning in the local model.

These are not implementation labels. They are governance decisions.

If the distinction is absent, a synchronized or externally sourced value can appear indistinguishable from an owned record. A convenient representation can outlive or diverge from its source. A platform team can become responsible for semantics it never had the mandate to define.

Boundaries make the model safer to extend

An explicit boundary does not constrain the value of ServiceNow. It makes extension more sustainable.

Teams can use representations of external knowledge without pretending that ownership has moved. They can use ServiceNow workflows while preserving specialist semantics in an architecture repository. They can expose a dependency without transferring ownership of the underlying contract. They can identify where conflicting sources require accountable resolution.

ServiceNow already provides substantial capabilities for integrating, relating and governing information across enterprise systems. ARC does not seek to replace those capabilities or introduce another universal schema. It addresses the architecture boundary around them: which source is authoritative for what, how externally owned knowledge should participate in a decision and what evidence supports the conclusion being used.

This produces a stronger shared model because users can understand its limits. Confidence comes not from claiming completeness, but from knowing what the model contains, where the information came from and what the model is trusted to support.

The best boundary for a strategic platform is not the smallest one. Nor is it the largest one technically possible. It is the boundary at which responsibility, meaning and operational control remain aligned.

When that boundary is explicit, ServiceNow can expand without silently turning every connection into a transfer of authority.
