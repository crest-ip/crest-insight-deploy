Where ARC complements ServiceNow

Part 3 of 5

Integration moves data. Architecture must preserve what the data means

Enterprise platforms are designed to integrate. ServiceNow provides extensive capabilities for connecting, contextualizing and governing information across enterprise systems, including through Workflow Data Fabric and its broader integration capabilities.

That makes information more available and useful across workflows. But when the same information is used as evidence for an architecture decision, another question becomes important: has the meaning required for that decision survived the connection?

A value can be stored, exposed or synchronized successfully while part of its architectural context remains elsewhere: who asserted it, when it was valid, which definition was used, whether it was observed or inferred and what should happen when another authoritative source disagrees.

The hidden decisions inside a connection

Every connection between information models contains architectural choices, whether or not they have been documented.

A many-to-many relationship may become one field. Several lifecycle states may be mapped into a smaller taxonomy. A source timestamp may be confused with the time of retrieval or synchronization. A proposed future-state object may appear indistinguishable from an operational fact.

The connection can therefore be technically successful while still changing the meaning available to an architecture decision.

Four controls help preserve that meaning:

  • Provenance: where did this statement originate, and can a reviewer inspect the source?
  • Time: when was it true, when was it retrieved and is it still current enough for this decision?
  • Knowledge state: is it observed, inferred, proposed, reviewed or accepted?
  • Authority: which source or accountable person resolves disagreement?

These controls do not need to burden every routine transaction. They become important when information from several sources is used as evidence to justify consequential change.

Connect is more than connectivity

ServiceNow already provides sophisticated capabilities for integrating and governing information. ARC does not seek to duplicate that integration layer.

ARC addresses the architecture question that remains when a decision crosses several legitimate sources of authority.

ServiceNow may provide portfolio and operational records. A specialist repository may provide model semantics and relationships. Documents may explain an exception or architectural rationale. An API catalogue may own the contract that a proposed change affects. Accountable people may still need to resolve interpretations that no system can establish on its own.

ARC assembles the relevant architecture context around the decision while keeping those distinctions visible.

This allows the architecture team to reason across sources without treating every connected value as equally authoritative. If two sources disagree, the conflict can be exposed for resolution. If a conclusion is inferred, it can remain distinguishable from an observed or accepted fact. If source information changes, its provenance provides the basis for determining which conclusions may need to be reconsidered.

The objective is not to slow integration down. It is to prevent connectivity from creating unsupported confidence.

ServiceNow becomes more useful when its information can participate in decisions that cross the platform boundary. Specialist sources become more useful when they can contribute context without surrendering their semantics or authority.

Integration answers whether information can be connected and used.

Architecture must also answer whether it still means what we think it means when we act on it.
