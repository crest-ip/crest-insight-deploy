When Finance corrects the same input again

Part 5 of 5

Start with one recurring correction

A business problem does not have to become a large mapping programme. Choose one recurring correction that several people recognise and that can be followed from beginning to end.

Make a first attempt together

  1. Choose a case. Take a recently handled invoice or another correction that recurs. Write down which information was missing and which work had to wait.
  2. Collect the evidence. Bring the original input, the additions and what the next person could see. Emails and local spreadsheets may also reveal how the work actually happened.
  3. Bring together the people who know. Ask the person who corrected the input and the person who created or handed it over to review the case together. Add systems expertise if the information transfer needs to be explained.
  4. Follow what happened. Mark where information was created, passed on and completed. Separate what you can demonstrate from what you still assume.

Compare with a reference

Use a coherent reference view to orient the discussion:

  • Which activities do we recognise?
  • Which inputs do we use?
  • Where is our version different?
  • Which exceptions matter to us?

The reference should not force the organisation into a standard answer. The differences are what you need to understand. If the question is still too broad, open a bounded detailed example.

End with a decision, not more documentation

The purpose is not more diagrams. It is a clear next step: clarify an information definition, change a handoff, test a system function, adjust responsibility or collect more facts. A useful result may also be ruling out a suspected cause.

Write down four things: the observation, the evidence, the remaining question and who owns the next step. Also decide when to follow up. A confirmed information gap may justify closer investigation; it is not yet a decision to purchase an integration.
