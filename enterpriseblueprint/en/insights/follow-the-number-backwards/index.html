When Finance corrects the same input again

Part 3 of 5

Follow the number backwards

Two reports show different sales figures. One comes from the CRM system and the other from the ERP system. Which one is correct?

The question may be premature. Sales may refer to accepted orders, delivered goods or invoiced amounts. Both reports may therefore be internally consistent while providing different answers.

Consider a hypothetical example: the CRM system shows an accepted order value of 100,000. For the same order, the ERP system shows an invoiced amount of 60,000. The remaining 40,000 is due to be delivered later. For simplicity, the amounts exclude VAT, returns and discounts.

The difference is not necessarily an error. Follow the number backwards before deciding what should be corrected.

1. The concept

Write down what the measure means in the decision where it is used. Does sales mean a customer commitment, an order, a delivery or recognised revenue? Are returns, discounts and credit notes included? In this example, we compare accepted order value with invoiced amount — two different measures.

2. The event

Connect the measure to what happened. An order was accepted on one date, delivery occurred on another and the invoice was created later. If the reports use different events, the difference may be one of timing or definition rather than a data error. Here, the full order has been accepted, but only part of it has been delivered and invoiced.

3. The source

Which system or document records the event? Who is accountable for the information? Document the field, selection, calculation and retrieval time. In the example, confirm that both reports concern the same order and comparison date.

4. The handoff

Follow how the information moves onwards. A process view shows what the next activity needs, but it does not automatically map your reporting fields to the events. You do that with your own sources.

5. The control

Finally, which control makes the number useful for the decision?

It may be a reconciliation, an approval or a requirement for complete supporting information. State who performs the control and what happens when it does not pass.

The chain can be summarised as:

Concept → event → source → handoff → control

It helps you determine whether the reports genuinely contradict each other. Perhaps the data needs to be corrected, the measure needs a clearer name, or the decision should use two different measures.

The aim is not to force a single number. It is to know which number the question requires and to be able to explain why.
