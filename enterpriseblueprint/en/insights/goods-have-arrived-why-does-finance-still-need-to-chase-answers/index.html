When Finance corrects the same input again

Part 4 of 5

The goods have arrived. Why does Finance still need to chase answers?

The purchase order exists. The goods have arrived and are already in use. The supplier invoice has been received. Finance still needs to ask what was actually delivered before the invoice can proceed.

In our hypothetical example, the goods have been received physically. The recipient may even have made a local note of the date and quantity. But the person reviewing the invoice has no accessible goods-receipt evidence linked to the purchase order. Someone therefore searches for the delivery note, contacts the buyer and reconstructs the answer.

Receiving and recording are not the same thing

The reference model distinguishes between two activities and two results:

  • Receive goods delivery results in a received goods delivery at the receiving location.
  • Record goods receipt results in a recorded goods receipt.

The distinction becomes concrete in the invoice case. The goods may be in the warehouse, and the recipient may know that ten packages arrived on Tuesday. That does not mean the next activity has documented goods-receipt evidence that can be used together with the purchase order and supplier invoice.

Ask two questions: Was receipt documented? Can the next activity use the information?

Read the model from the event to the next decision

Read the extract in three steps:

  1. Start with receipt. Compare Ta emot varuleverans with what actually happened in your case.
  2. Follow the evidence. Examine what exists before and after Redovisa varumottagning. Physical receipt and recorded evidence are different things.
  3. Check what the recipient needs. In the reference, the recorded goods receipt and supplier invoice meet at Fastställa leverantörsskuld.

The reference view shows the structure of ordinary work. The hypothetical interruption is not drawn into it. Your task is to compare the model with a real case and mark where the chain can no longer be followed.

Prepare a shared review

Bring:

  • one invoice case that required additional contact or completion;
  • the purchase order and goods-receipt evidence that existed;
  • the information Finance could see when the invoice arrived;
  • someone from Finance and the buyer or recipient;
  • when necessary, someone who can explain the transfer of information between systems.

You do not need to map the entire purchasing flow. Follow one case and distinguish facts from assumptions.

From question to a basis for action

A worksheet after the review might look like this:

Observation in the example

The goods have been received. Usable goods-receipt evidence is missing when Finance reviews the invoice.

Evidence to obtain in a real case

The purchase order, goods-receipt evidence and any local note or history. Confirm with the person who received the delivery what was documented, where and when.

Decision to prepare if the observation is confirmed

Who should record receipt, when should it happen, and how do we ensure that the next activity can use the information?

What this does not yet justify

A new integration. That question becomes relevant only if receipt is documented but the evidence still does not reach the person handling the invoice.

The individual case may still need to be resolved. The worksheet distinguishes the temporary handling from the change that may reduce recurring work.

About the example

The example is hypothetical. The reference view helps you investigate where usable goods-receipt evidence may be missing, but it is not a prescribed model for your organisation. It describes neither a specific customer nor a specific system configuration.
