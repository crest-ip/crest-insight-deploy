When Finance corrects the same input again

Part 1 of 5

The correction happens in Finance. The problem may start earlier.

An invoice cannot be matched to the goods received. An amount does not agree with last week’s report. An accrual needs to be adjusted again.

Finance resolves the situation so that the month can be closed. But Finance may simply be where an earlier ambiguity becomes visible. The input may have originated in purchasing, delivery, inventory or another operational step.

Choose a correction that recurs

Do not start with the entire close. Choose one activity that people recognise from several periods. Describe what Finance has to correct or complete:

  • Which piece of information is missing?
  • What has to be checked?
  • Which answer must be obtained from another part of the organisation?

Then trace the input backwards. Do not ask only which system it came from. Ask which business event would make the information true and useful.

For a supplier invoice, that may mean examining the relationship between the purchase order, the goods received and the invoice. What does the next step need to receive, when should the information exist, and how can the recipient determine that it is fit for use?

Distinguish necessary control from reconstructed work

Not all manual work is waste. A judgement may require experience, and a control may be deliberate. What you want to find is the work created because the same information has to be interpreted, completed or recorded again.

Use a shared picture

A reference model gives Finance, operations and IT a structure for following the same case. Compare which inputs exist, who uses them and where someone has to ask a question or re-enter information. That makes it easier to define the next investigation.
